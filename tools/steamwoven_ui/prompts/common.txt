STEAMWOVEN INTERFACE KIT - COMMON RULES (applies to every prompt of the library)

You refine interface art of a steampunk-gothic role-playing game of the late 1800s (brass, riveted iron, dark streaked wood, aged paper, glass vials, clockwork). The refinement raises only the resolution and the surface texture (fine wood grain, light scratches, wear, patina, paper fibres): the same objects, layout, shapes, palette, brightness and contrast as the ORIGINAL, never a different design. Add nothing the ORIGINAL does not show: no extra ornament, no extra shine or gloss, no extra detail. The whole look stays dusty, worn and muted, exactly as dull as the ORIGINAL.

Fixed rules:
1. Layout truth is the ORIGINAL reference image: keep every element where it is, with the same size relations, the same reading order and the same purpose. Do not move, add, remove or merge elements. (A prompt that says which layers to leave empty, such as a strip or a window background without its buttons, overrides this for those layers only.) When the ORIGINAL is given as the IMAGE TO EDIT, refine that image: raise its detail and resolution, do not change its colour or its shape.
2. For context images, window backgrounds and plates: one 4:3 composition, the whole centred interface. Nothing continues beyond the picture edges: no extra wood, no frame bars, no side panels, no vignette outside the art, no backdrop wider than the picture. (The material sheet and single parts have the size and aspect their own prompt gives.)
3. No text. Do not draw any letters, numbers, words, glyphs of a script, labels, signatures or watermarks. Text fields stay empty (dark field, blank paper line, empty plaque): the game draws all text with its own fonts. Symbols that are pictures (a key, a gear, a heart) are allowed only where the ORIGINAL has them.
4. Palette and materials: keep the ORIGINAL palette: the same hue, saturation, lightness and contrast in every region; do not brighten, darken, saturate or add contrast (the result, reduced to 800 x 600 and blurred, is compared with the ORIGINAL by a tone check and is redrawn when it drifts). Brass is dull, darkened and matte, never gold, never polished or bright yellow. Glass is slightly cloudy, never clean or bright. Wood has the tone of the ORIGINAL. When a MATERIAL SHEET reference is given, every surface must look cut from it (same wood tone and grain scale, same brass, iron and paper). Colour-lock to it: do not drift in hue, saturation or lightness between parts. Without a sheet, take the materials from the ORIGINAL reference.
5. Light, shadow and highlights are as in the ORIGINAL; add no new gloss, no specular shine and no glow. Metal edges stay matte, wood keeps a satin finish, not a gloss.
6. Painted, not photographic: no lens blur, no noise grain, no JPEG-like artefacts, no hard outlines drawn around shapes.
7. Output: one PNG, the exact aspect asked for in the prompt. If you cannot render the exact pixel size, render the nearest size with the same aspect, as large as you can; it is fitted afterwards. Transparent parts use a transparent background (real alpha, no checkerboard, no coloured matte) with a clean edge and no halo.
8. Never include real-world logos, people or brands.
